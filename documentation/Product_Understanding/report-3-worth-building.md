## Two questions, two answers

"Is it worth competing?" depends on what the lab is for. The Acies program guidance is
specific: the labs are *"a core GTM asset"* — *"working, browser-based interactive demos"*
designed to *"open conversations with Senior Director / VP-level leaders."* That changes the
answer.

**As a software product competing with o9, SAP and Aera? No.** The category is owned by
Gartner Leaders with hundreds of millions in funding, agentic AI already shipping, and
enterprise data integrations the lab doesn't have.

**As the go-to-market lab it was specified to be? Yes.** Incumbents take 12–24 months to show
value. A VP can see the "after" picture in this lab in minutes — and the strength of the
incumbents makes that more useful, not less.

## Key findings

1. **Every one of the lab's five capabilities already has well-funded specialists.** Portfolio
   health, launch readiness, profitability, SKU rationalization and market signals are each
   covered by established vendors. There is no unserved capability here.
2. **The "agentic decision layer" is already a named, contested category.** Gartner published
   its first Magic Quadrant for Decision Intelligence Platforms in 2026. Aera Technology is a
   Leader, with Hershey among its CPG customers. o9, Kinaxis and SAP all ship AI agents today.
3. **What incumbents can't do is show value quickly.** Enterprise planning deployments
   reportedly run 12–24 months, with three-year costs in the millions. That buying friction is
   precisely the gap a demonstration lab fills.
4. **Funded AI-native entrants win by going narrow.** The 2026 rounds went to companies solving
   one painful workflow — Glimpse raised $35M for retail deductions alone. None raised to build
   a broad five-module suite.

## The landscape

Seven segments, ordered by how directly each overlaps with the lab. Vendor-reported claims are
marked.

### 1. Integrated planning suites — HIGHEST OVERLAP
*The incumbents. Enterprise-wide planning platforms that already cover portfolio, supply and financial decisions.*

- **o9 Solutions** — Valued at $3.7B, with $536M raised from KKR and General Atlantic;
  subscription revenue grew 37% in 2024. A Leader in **both** 2026 Gartner Magic Quadrants for
  supply chain planning, and one of only two planning vendors also placed in the new Decision
  Intelligence quadrant. Customers include AB InBev and PepsiCo.
- **Kinaxis** — A Leader in both 2026 planning quadrants — highest on ability to execute in
  discrete industries. Launched Maestro Agents in 2025, Maestro Agent Studio in 2026, with an
  agent marketplace planned. *(Ranking claim published by the vendor.)*
- **SAP Integrated Business Planning** — Three Joule AI agents for supply chain; IBP's AI
  capabilities targeted for general availability in Q2 2026. Directly relevant to consumer
  goods: promotional price-pack decisions now flow automatically into supply plans.
- **Oracle, Blue Yonder, OMP** — Oracle is a Leader in both 2026 planning quadrants. Blue
  Yonder is a Leader for discrete and a Visionary for process industries. OMP placed highest on
  both axes for process industries — the quadrant that covers food and beverage.

### 2. Decision intelligence — CLOSEST CONCEPT
*The closest match to the "Acies AgenticBus" concept: AI agents that recommend and execute business decisions.*

- **Aera Technology** — Named a Leader in Gartner's first Magic Quadrant for Decision
  Intelligence Platforms, January 2026. Hershey adopted Aera's AI for supply chain resilience.
  Aera also names Mars, Kraft Heinz and Unilever as customers, and plans self-assembling agent
  teams for 2026. *(Customer list and roadmap published by the vendor.)*

### 3. Revenue growth management — PARTIAL OVERLAP
*Pricing, promotion and margin tools — overlapping the lab's Profitability Tree.*

- **UpClear, Visualfabriq, Infor, LatentView** — UpClear's Blue RGM lists Danone, Utz, Vita
  Coco and Twinings as users. Infor entered the category by acquiring the CPG consultancy
  Acumen. Visualfabriq builds bottom-up P&L forecasts for commercial leaders. *(Customer names
  published by the vendor.)*

### 4. SKU rationalization tools — PARTIAL OVERLAP
*Point solutions for the lab's SKU Rationalisation Simulator.*

- **AnswerRocket, ThroughPut AI, RELEX Solutions** — AnswerRocket reports helping a global
  beauty company cut its portfolio by 40%. ThroughPut AI reports profit gains for a European
  retailer within 90 days. RELEX runs market-basket analysis against consumer decision trees
  and spoilage data. *(Outcomes reported by the vendors.)*

### 5. Product lifecycle management — ADJACENT
*Formulation, compliance and launch workflow. Overlaps Launch Readiness — but the brief places full PLM out of scope.*

- **Centric Software** — Confirmed in June 2026 as Dassault Systèmes' PLM standard for
  consumer goods and retail. Positions itself on faster time-to-market for reformulations and
  launches, and markets a visual-boards product for SKU rationalization.

### 6. Market measurement — DATA SOURCE
*The data the lab's Signal Board would consume — suppliers, not rivals.*

- **NIQ, Circana, Numerator** — Three companies dominate measurement of what consumers bought;
  most consumer goods companies pay at least one of them. Circana has acquired Nielsen's
  marketing mix modeling business.

### 7. AI-native entrants, 2026 — SIGNAL
*Newly funded companies — useful mainly for the pattern in what investors backed.*

- **Glimpse** — $35M Series A led by Andreessen Horowitz in March 2026, $52M in total. One
  workflow: AI agents that review retail deductions and file disputes, for more than 200
  brands.
- **Conveo · Certo** — Conveo raised a $50M Series A in September 2026 for AI consumer
  research. Certo raised a $4M seed in May 2026 for regulatory compliance in beauty and
  consumer goods.

No single vendor puts all five in front of a VP Product in one place, without an
implementation. But the integrated suites come closest — and that gap is a **sales** gap, not
a product gap. It's the problem of letting a buyer see the outcome before committing to a
multi-year project.

## Why it can't compete as a product

| Barrier | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Capital | o9 alone has raised $536M. The lab is a capstone build. |
| The category is taken | Gartner now ranks decision intelligence as its own quadrant, with named Leaders. |
| Agents already ship | Kinaxis, SAP, o9 and Aera all have production AI agents. The lab's "agents" are keyword-matched replies. |
| No data integration | Real value depends on ERP, point-of-sale and syndicated data. The lab has no backend, connectors or model inference. |
| Maturity | Reports 1 and 2 found two competing revenue baselines, four empty KPI strips and twelve tabs against a five-tab spec. |

## Why it is worth building as a GTM lab

| Reason | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Buying is slow and expensive | Deployments reportedly run 12–18 months for Kinaxis and 12–24 for o9 and SAP IBP, with mid-market three-year costs of $2.5–6M and hidden integration costs adding 30–50%. A VP needs to see the outcome first. |
| Acies can stay neutral | Every suite vendor sells its own platform. A consultancy can show the decision layer working across whichever stack a client already owns. |
| The story is now credible | With Gartner recognizing decision intelligence and every leader shipping agents, the "How This Evolves" narrative describes where the market is going — not speculation. |
| A scoped pilot is a proven shape | Glimpse shows the pattern: one painful, measurable workflow. The lab can open exactly that conversation. |

**Read the cost figures with care.** The deployment timelines and costs come from Horizon
Solutions, which sells a competing planning product and has an interest in portraying
incumbents as slow and costly. The direction is widely consistent; treat the exact figures as
indicative.

## Recommendation

| Decision | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Build it? | **Yes** — as the GTM lab the program specifies. |
| Sell it as software? | **No.** Don't position it against o9, SAP, Kinaxis or Aera. |
| How to position it | As the decision layer a VP sees on top of the planning stack and market data they already pay for — complementary to incumbents, not a replacement. |
| If a product is ever wanted | Don't build a five-module suite. Pick one narrow, measurable workflow — for example, the SKU sunset business case — and integrate with existing platforms. |

**What would make the lab better at its actual job:**
- **One set of numbers.** A VP who spots $473M on one screen and $851M on another stops
  trusting the demo. See report 1.
- **Five tabs.** The brief requires it, and a shorter tour lands harder. See report 2.
- **Make the CTA work.** The Consulting CTA is where a GTM asset converts — its three buttons
  currently do nothing.
- **Name the integration story.** Show the side panel positioning Acies across planning suites
  and syndicated data, since that's where a real engagement would sit.
- **Remove the real trademark.** "Coca-Cola 500ml" appears in the SKU list of a demo shown to
  senior leaders.

## Limits of this research

- Gartner rankings are quoted from vendor announcements; the reports themselves are paywalled.
- Customer lists, outcomes and roadmaps are marked where the vendor is the only source.
- Market-size estimates were too inconsistent to use: one firm puts supply chain planning
  software at $12.5B in 2024, another at $0.66B in 2026.
- This is desk research as of September 2026, not interviews with buyers.
